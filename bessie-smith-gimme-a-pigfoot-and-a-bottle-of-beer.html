Student writing · Track 06

Bessie Smith — Gimme a Pigfoot and a Bottle of Beer

STUDENT WRITINGOpen original · Google Docs

“Gimme a Pigfoot and a Bottle of Beer” by Bessie Smith played on repeat as I soaked up its infectious energy. The song is irresistible, making it almost impossible to sit still. It is the kind of song to bring life to history. In fact, while listening, it feels as if I am able to open a time capsule and reflect on the spirit, resilience, and creativity of Black culture during the Harlem Renaissance (Ocran). Even though I am not physically in a Harlem speakeasy, it is easy to imagine people dancing and singing along in the crowded smoky club.

The powerful effect of this song is a result of Smith’s lyrics, instrumentals, and overall composition. Immediately after pressing play for the first time, the flirtatious lively piano jumped out at me, stirring excitement within me as it exuded a playful swing feel (0-0:12). Additionally, Smith’s vocal entry surprised me with a conversational tone before she commanded my attention with the deep timbre of her contralto voice (0:10-0:20). Throughout my first listen, I was struck by the strong rhythms, soulful vocals, and rich storytelling of the song.

After listening to the song repeatedly, I was able to identify the musical components which make the song swing and come alive. A staple of blues is the 12-bar blues structure, which creates the cyclical momentum of this song (Ocran). This 12-bar structure gives the song a steady, driving foundation while the phrasing, vocals, and instrumentation fills (1:48-2:37) keep the song dynamic and engaging. The format of the 12-bar structure allows for the call-and-response between Smith’s vocals and the horn section (1:38-1:47) (Ocran). Additionally, the syncopation of the piano, horns, and vocals marked by unevenly played eighth notes in a loping, lilting groove further contribute to the loose swing feel, almost as if the musicians are feeding off the energy of a party (1:10-1:15). Smith also features blue notes, which she uses to bend pitches in order elicit the celebratory emotional effect of the song (0:57-1:05) (Ocran).

I am drawn to music that has strong rhythms, soulful vocals, and rich storytelling, which perfectly characterizes this song. My affinity for this song says that I appreciate music with historical and emotional depth. I feel that everyone would enjoy this song, as it still feels vibrant and can fit into the modern world. Through cocktail lounges and bars, the relaxed but energetic feel is perfect for a night out.

Upon final reflection, this song makes me think about how people define happiness in a variety of ways. For some, happiness is luxury and prestige, and for others it is as simple as a pigfoot and a bottle of beer.
