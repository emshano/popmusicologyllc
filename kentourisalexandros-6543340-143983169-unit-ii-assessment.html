MUSC 1420

Unit II Assessment – Alexandros Kentouris

Introduction

“Keep on the Sunny Side” by The Carter Family is a classic country song recorded in 1928, whose significance in the hillbilly/country music genre and lasting impact on the broader American folk music scene is undeniable. More thoroughly, the song's lyrics, musical elements, and characteristic performance choices contribute to its uplifting effect and its aforementioned historical and cultural importance.

Lyrics

Beginning the analysis of the song with its rich musical elements, it is worth scrutinizing its rather simple lyrics, since they attain the artist’s goal of reinforcing the listener’s optimism and resilience. For instance, first two lines at 0:18-0:25 reassure the listeners that even if “There's a dark and a troubled side of life” there is “…a bright and a sunny side too”. Additionally, the gospel influences of “Keep on the Sunny Side” can be observed in the lines “Let us trust in our Saviour…keep us, every one, in His care” at 2:06-2:13, indicating the strong Christian influences in early country music. The lyrics encourage a positive outlook on everyday life despite the myriads of hardships that are faced, resonating with listeners during the Great Depression who oftentimes provided manual labour in the agricultural industry of the South. Originating from blues music that was popular with the African American population at the start of the 20th century, the term “hillbilly music” was first coined by music labels in the 1920s, so as to separate country and blues and more effectively target racially different audiences. “Hillbilly” is a derogatory term that originally referred to southern backwoods’ inhabitants, particularly in Appalachia. Nonetheless, even if I would not consider myself a member of the song’s initially chosen target audience, I contend that the song’s general semantic field of positive thinking and perseverance still holds relevance today. Regarding the song’s extramusical elements, I personally listen to Greek folk music (λαϊκό), which has similar tropes due to the poor living conditions of 20th century Greeks, so I believe that my exposure to this music genre contributed to my enjoyment of the country song. This song inspires me to overcome my struggles. Country music is still very popular in the US, especially in the South and Midwest.

Melody and harmony

Moving on to the rest of the musical elements, the song’s simple and highly memorable melody makes it easier for listeners to sing along. The band’s signature close harmonies provide additional warmth and depth. For example, at around 0:33-0:45, the chorus begins with: "Keep on the sunny side, always on the sunny side, keep on the sunny side of life...". Therefore, it can be observed that the harmony is consonant, with Maybelle Carter closely following the melody and the harmonization staying within a narrow interval range, often in thirds and fifths (characteristic of early country and Appalachian folk music). As a result, the uplifting nature and intimacy of the song are accentuated, highlighting its theme of hope.

Rhythm, instrumentation, and other techniques

A characteristic of country music, the song’s steady, waltz-like rhythm with guitar accompaniment (Carter scratch technique) works well with the repetition of the chorus in reinforcing the song’s hopeful message and enabling communal singing.

References to Course Materials

Connecting to the lecture on country music, the song is a powerful example of the role of early country music in shaping the American musical identity and building on its ties to folk traditions. In fact, the influence of rural life and folk storytelling are the product of blues and country music emerging from the struggles of working-class Americans during and after the Reconstruction period and as a result of the Great Migration.

Conclusion

To conclude, the cultural significance and aim of the song to procure hope and perseverance to its listeners make sense when placed into the historical context and when considering the socioeconomic changes occurring at the time of its recording. However, it should be noted that while the song originated in an earlier era, its message and musical qualities continue to resonate today, proving the timeless power of music to empower and inspire.
