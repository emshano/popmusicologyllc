Student writing · Track 09

The Ronettes — Be My Baby

STUDENT WRITINGOpen original · Google Docs

I listened to “Be My Baby” by the Ronettes (1963), which comes from a time in musical history when girl groups were very popular. I will examine the musical elements, extramusical elements, and the historical and contextual elements of this piece.

Musical Elements

The lyrics of this song align with the expected “girl talk” that most girl groups of the 1960s used. The song consists of the lead, Veronica Bennett aka “Ronnie”, backed by her sister Estelle Bennet and her cousin Nedra Talley. The “girl group sound” is evident in this piece with the presence of young, energetic, “sweet sounding” female voices featuring close vocal harmonies and supportive percussion in the back, including claps. In this song, Ronnie pleads to a man, attempting to convince him to “be her baby”. Ronnie sings, “I'll make you happy, baby, just wait and see / for every kiss you give me, I'll give you three.” The lyrics of this song convey the message that women only think about pleasing men. Through the lyrics of this song and the picture it creates, the Ronettes present themselves in a manner appealing to men of that time who valued glamor, sophistication, and innocence. The song opens with an iconic drumming pattern that goes “boom-ba-boom-chang”, and has been used in other songs such as “Don’t Worry Baby” by the Beach Boys. The song uses a 4/4 time signature and has a flowy rhythm that is perfect for dancing. Ronnie’s voice carries a strong melody, while Estelle and Nedra harmonize in support, singing repeated “ooo”, “ah”, “be my, be my baby” in response to Ronnie’s melody. Instrumentation for this song includes percussion (drums, tambourines, piano, maracas, and more), as well as guitars, bass, and a string orchestra at 1:39, where the melody is played instead of sung. A layered instrumentation technique is used in collaboration with the harmonies sung by Estelle and Nedra to create a supportive backing for Ronnie’s lead vocals. The Ronettes have a calm yet exciting performance style, with Ronnie standing slightly in front, Estelle and Nedra standing slightly behind singing the backing vocals. Throughout their performance, the Ronettes always keep a smile on their faces and dance in a synced fashion that makes the crowd want to join them in dancing.

Extramusical Elements

To me, “Be My Baby” is a light-hearted song that makes me want to dance. This is a pop song, which aligns with what I enjoy listening to. When I hear “Be My Baby”, I instantly recognize it and want to sing along to the catchy lyrics, however I cannot necessarily pinpoint where and when I was introduced to this song and why it brings a sense of comfort. I have definitely heard this song in various shows/movies when the 1960s are being referenced, and I believe I have also heard it in stores.

Historical and Contextual Elements

“Be My Baby” was released in 1963 during a time where girl groups were boosting in popularity. This song was a big hit when it was released and reached number 2 on Billboard’s top 100 in October 1963. Today this song is still very popular as it is a classic that represents the 1960s rock and roll girl group era and is 22 on the Rolling Stone’s 500 greatest songs. This song has been included in a wide variety of TV shows and movies. In my opinion, this should continue to be included in TV shows and movies to create a sense of nostalgia for the 1960s and to represent the “young love” era of adolescence/early adulthood.
