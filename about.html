Scholarship, in conversation.

PopMusicology is a music education company specializing in public musicology. As an independent tape label, we bridge rigorous music scholarship, popular music histories, public-facing criticism, and physical media archiving.

We advocate slow listening and encourage the queer intimacy that certain media formats can provide. Libraries, music collections, and archives inform our work, alongside the Akan traditional value of Sankofa.

Our mission is to encourage, curate, and use the occasion of cassette listening to critique and release popular music of all genres and styles.

01

Kwame Kruw Ocran Founder

Kwame K. Ocran is just some guy from the Bronx who happens to spend an inordinate amount of time thinking about pop divas, cassette tape, and finding creative ways to engage in public musicological discourse. Currently based in Philadelphia, he is a PhD candidate (ABD) in Historical Musicology at the University of Pennsylvania. His dissertation research, Taped Entanglement, bridges music criticism with cassette culture to explore collage as an intervention against exhaustive media narratives. His recent scholarly work examines the critical afterlife of Mariah Carey’s Glitter, the methodology of “slow listening” applied to Lady Gaga’s Mayhem, and the grey market of Black popular listening and recording in the late twentieth century.

He has presented his research at the American Musicological Society, Harvard’s Graduate Student Music Conference, and PopCon. A committed public humanist and educator, Kwame has taught courses like “Thinking About Popular Music” at UPenn and served as an adjunct instructor within the CUNY system. He serves as a Graduate Fellow for the Africana Studies Summer Institute and directs the PopMusicology Archival Residency, mentoring student researchers in digitization and duplication workflows. His work is supported by the UPenn Department of Music, the William J. Fontaine Academic Fellowship, and the Center for Africana Studies Harold J. Haskins Research Grant. A 2010 Brooklyn Technical High School graduate, he completed his undergraduate studies in Philosophy and Critical Race and Gender Studies as a CUNY Baccalaureate at the Macaulay Honors College. Kwame also holds a Master’s in Liberal Studies from the CUNY Graduate Center with a concentration in American Performance.

Beyond the academy, Kwame runs PopMusicology, LLC, an independent boutique record label and research collective. He founded PopMusicology to contribute to conversations in music criticism, cassette culture, and public musicology. As an analog preservationist, he maintains a media collection of over 1,500 cassettes, 650 CDs, and 200 vinyl with a primary focus on pop, R&B, soul and divas. Whether he is recording his favorite performances onto tape, developing opportunities for public audiences to “listen slowly,” or releasing physical media for independent artists, his practice remains rooted in treating physical archiving as a vital act of community care.

03

Ian Sadock Music Theory Consultant, Digitization Intern

Ian Sadock is an award-winning music theorist, pianist, and composer from Philadelphia whose research focuses on the oral traditions and improvised theories of music in their diaspora from Western and Sub-Saharan Africa to the Americas. He is a PhD student in Music Theory at Temple University, where his recent work explores the geometry of John Coltrane’s multi-tonal language, pedagogical methods of implementing Afro-Cuban Batá models in the music theory classroom, reharmonization techniques in American popular song and the jazz idiom, and the cognitive-affective architecture of emotional arousal in collective political chanting. He has presented his research at the Society for Ethnomusicology’s Cognitive Ethnomusicology Special Interest Group, the University of Cambridge, and Temple University. He also writes about improvisation, psychoacoustics, film criticism, politics, and AI in his Substack, Time No Changes.

Sadock has held teaching positions at The Settlement Music School, The University of the Arts, Cheltenham Township Adult School, and Greensleeves Music. He serves on the Board of Directors at Jazz Bridge, a Philadelphia nonprofit supporting jazz and blues musicians, and chairs its Development Committee. He holds a Master of Music in Jazz Studies from The University of the Arts and a Bachelor of Music in piano performance, with a concentration in jazz, from West Chester University, as well as a Bachelor of Science in Cell & Molecular Biology and national certification as an Advanced EMT.

His honors include several Illuminate the Arts Grants, two Boysie Lowery Living Jazz Residencies, the Dr. B Stimson Carrow Tribute Award, the Esther Boyer Griswold Award, the Michael Falcone Jazz Scholarship, and first place in the Nelly Berman 25th Anniversary Golden Key Competition.

A performing and recording artist reviewed by JazzTimes and All About Jazz, Sadock can be heard most recently on the album Boleros de Tite by the group Taller Ciudad Perdida. He has performed at the Kimmel Center, the Philadelphia Clef Club of Jazz & Performing Arts, TIME, Chris’s Jazz Cafe, Heritage, and SOUTH Jazz Kitchen, among others.
