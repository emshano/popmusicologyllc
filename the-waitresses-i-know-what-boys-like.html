Student writing · Track 02

The Waitresses — I Know What Boys Like

STUDENT WRITINGOpen original · Google Docs

New wave is a subgenre of rock music which encompasses punk rock, pop music, synth pop, and funk among other genres. Influenced by happier 60s pop and the implementation of synth pop that arose, New Wave enjoyed popularity on the merits of songs like The Waitresses’ “I Know What Boys Like”. The band hails from Akron, Ohio.

The popular single was written by guitarist Chris Butler in 1978, who was a member of the rock band known as Tin Huey. The Waitresses were a side project of Butler’s at the time. Butler moved to New York City and released the song in 1980 to little success. The original release did not chart on the Billboard Hot 100 or garner much attention in the US. However, the song did land Butler a deal with ZE Records which allowed for him to put together a lineup for the Waitresses. The original members were lead vocalist Patty Donahue, saxophonist Mars Williams, drummer Billy Ficca, keyboardist Dan Klayman, bassist Dave Hofstra, and Ariel Warner on backing vocals. With the lineup in place and Tin Huey beginning to disband in the early 1980s (officially 1982), Butler was able to focus his full attention on the Waitresses. On January 11th, 1982, the Waitresses released their debut album Wasn’t Tomorrow Wonderful? Which included a re-recorded version of “I Know What Boys Like”. This version was re-released as a single a year later and reached a peak of number 62 on the Billboard Hot 100 and number 23 on Billboard’s Top Tracks chart. While the Waitresses disbanded soon after in 1983, this song remains their most popular and has been referred to as one of the greatest “one-hit wonder songs” of all time.

Upon repeated listens, the song appears to “taunt” men and issue a sort of takedown into the male gaze. This is especially interesting when considering that a man (Chris Butler) wrote the song. With Donahue’s sneering delivery of lines such as “I know what guys want/I see them looking/I make them want me/I like to tease them/They want to touch me/I never let them” (0:15-0:31), there is a reclamation of power for women through the song. I personally have a very feminist read of the song which is something that I enjoy about the song. I find that the lyrics dive deep into the objectification of women that we face by men. I think that women are very often sexualized by men and with the lyrics of the song, it is clear that Donahue is aware of that and the way men perceive her as an “object” in society. However, she and the listeners simultaneously reclaim power by saying how they would never allow men to approach them and how she gets a kick out of their subsequent disappointment. She sings, “they get so angry/like pouty children/denied their candy” (1:26-1:33). Interestingly, Donahue sounds bored and as though she is putting a minimal amount of effort into the delivery of the song itself but the hook of “I know what boys like/Boys like me” is nevertheless extremely catchy and characteristic of new wave “quirkiness” accompanied by eclectic guitar and saxophone. I love the instrumentation of the song coupled with the lyrics. I think it makes for a very catchy listen and one that goes well with the gritty guitars of the instrumentation.

Something I think about when listening to this song is the Glee cover performed in the 2nd season. While perhaps an unlikely thing for my mind to be drawn to, I find that the scene perfectly encapsulates the message of the song with lead singer ‘Zizes’ empowering herself in front of men in the Glee club that were objectifying her. Considering this and the fact that the Glee version of the song was the first time I had heard it, it feels very nostalgic for me. Therefore, I think I could see myself listening to the song while in my childhood bedroom, perhaps over a holiday break or sometime in the fall as I am reconnecting with a more angsty and younger version of myself when I stay at home. I have quite some love for this song and I would say it has a similar sound to a lot of bands I listened to when I was younger. For example, I absolutely adored The Talking Heads and the song “Don’t Worry About the Government” by that band reminds me a lot of the sonics of “I Know What Boys Like.”
