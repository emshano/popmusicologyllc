Student writing · Track 08

Alberta Hunter — The Darktown Strutters’ Ball

STUDENT WRITINGOpen original · Google Docs

Alberta Hunter’s energy and voice in “The Darktown Strutters’ Ball” are impeccable, in spite of her being over 80 when she recorded this song. The song is undeniably jazzy, and fitting with its lyrics (3:44 - “I want to be there when the band starts playin’”), seems to be excellent dancing music, regardless of whether the vocals or horns are leading. I’ve listened to it repeatedly for the sake of this assignment, and while I probably wouldn’t play the song on my own time, I can’t deny that it’s great. My own aversions to this song are simply matters of personal preference. The song’s slow and quite bare-bones (I counted five total instruments at 1:48, being Hunter’s singing, a piano, drums, and two different horns). Though the advancements in recording technology that allowed for more complex pieces didn’t exist at the beginning of Hunter’s career, they’d been around for decades by the time this song was created, meaning the number of instruments was a conscious choice. I don’t normally mind the slowness or simplicity, but I usually like for them to be building up to something grand towards the end of the piece. These aren’t knocks against the song itself; it’s a jazz standard for dances, it’s not meant to be complicated or intense. My opinions are merely indicative of taste; the only music genre that I’d say I generally enjoy is rock. There are still many songs from other genres that I really like, but those remain exceptions rather than the rule. There are multiple reasons for this, but it’s mostly because I like electric guitar and synth.

Hunter had an interesting career timeline, having been a star from the 1920s to the 50s, before working in nursing for two decades and then proceeding to make a comeback to singing in the 70s. She was alive when jazz developed out of Tin Pan Alley. She recorded “The Darktown Strutters’ Ball” in 1978, when she was about 83 years old. One might assume that she would attempt to mimic her singing voice from decades prior, but Hunter made no attempt to hide her age, sounding gravelly while not losing an ounce of talent. This is my favorite detail about the song; Hunter is unapologetically herself, and she sounds excellent while doing it. This is inspirational to me, and the most moving element of the song. I also think that it proves that in music, there’s no such thing as expiration dates. For every DJ at a prom to have George Michaels’ “Careless Whisper” in their set for the perfect slow dance, it makes perfect sense to have “The Darktown Strutters’ Ball” for a faster number, no matter what year it is.
