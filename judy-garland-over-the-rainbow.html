Judy Garland — Over the Rainbow

By Gabby Thomas

STUDENT WRITINGOpen original · Google Docs

I chose “Over the Rainbow” by Judy Garland for my musical analysis. “Over the Rainbow” was released in 1939, during the Tin Pan Alley (TPA) era of music. Similar to many TPA songs during the era, it follows the AABA musical format and possesses many of the characteristics common in songs during this period. The song’s structure is simple with a fairly predictable pattern due to its homophonic texture. The orchestra does not overpower the primary melody, so Garland’s vocals are the focal point of the performance. For the majority of the song, the tempo remains consistent. However, when Garland sings “someday I’ll wish upon a star, and wake up where the clouds are far behind me…” the tempo slightly increases for approximately two verses. However, the tempo soon returns to its original speed. The song is technically challenging because it includes several sharp interval changes, but Garland’s tone and dynamic remain even. She executes the interval leaps seamlessly, and it does not seem rushed. Though the melodic patterns are relatively simple, the lyrics are poetic and hopeful. The metaphor of “troubles melt[ing] like lemon drops” evokes vivid imagery and successfully conveys the song’s message. Because the lyrics and message are not provocative, the song was easily marketable. Additionally, since it was featured in The Wizard of Oz, it reached a large audience of people.

While this song does not align with my general preferences in genre or style, I always appreciated it because of Judy Garland’s excellent vocal performance which is worthy of study. Though she sings the song very simply, without much vocal improvisation, she injects emotion into it. My mom is a huge fan of Judy Garland and introduced me to her work, both on film and in music. “Over the Rainbow” is one of my favorite performances of hers, and I think the song’s hopeful message about daring to dream and leaving your current circumstances resonates with a large number of people, including me. I think that this song can be enjoyed almost anywhere. It has cemented itself as an iconic piece of music, which is constantly referenced, even today.
