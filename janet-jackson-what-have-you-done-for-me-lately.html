Student writing · Track 04

Janet Jackson — What Have You Done For Me Lately?

STUDENT WRITINGOpen original · Google Docs

Introduction

In 1986, Janet Jackson released “What Have You Done for Me Lately” as the first single from her third studio album, Control. This album blended her musical uniqueness and innovation with her thematic boldness, which definitely contributed to why Control became such a massive commercial success. Within Control, the songs introduced a fresh style that blended funk, disco, R&B, and pop. As the name of the album implies, Jackson placed an emphasis on independence and personal empowerment. Control sold over 10 million copies worldwide, topped both the US R&B and pop album charts, and spent 91 weeks on the R&B chart.

The unique style evident throughout Control is notable even in the very first seconds of “What Have You Done For Me Lately.” The song starts with a 15-second dialogue between Janet and her girlfriend discussing relationship issues that immediately immerses listeners into the story the song will tell. It sets a relatable, casual, and intimate tone and made me feel as if I was overhearing a private conversation. Using her television background (Good Times & The Jacksons) to create a ‘mini-scene’ before the music actually begins definitely helped Janet differentiate herself.

Musical Elements

The repetitive chorus, “What have you done for me lately? Ooh, ooh, ooh, yeah,” (0:54) was an immediate hook that makes the song catchy and instantly memorable. The repetition throughout the song reinforces the central theme of questioning a partner's commitment.

The song is constructed in the key of F minor, which inherently carries a sense of tension, frustration, and assertive energy which matches the song's lyrical theme of relationship dissatisfaction. The beat is also seemingly aggressive and syncopated, with a sharp, staccato rhythm. The 4/4 time signature also emphasizes off-beats, creating a sense of “interruption” that reflects the song's challenging message. Each beat sounds like a musical finger-wagging, reinforcing the “what have you done” narrative. Musically, the track sounds like a sonic argument. The sharp percussion, Jackson's crisp vocal delivery, and the minor key all come together to create a sound of powerful criticism.

Historical / Contextual Elements

The production of “What Have You Done for Me Lately” also came about interestingly. Janet had thought she was finished recording the Control album when John McClain requested one more uptempo song. Upon returning to Minneapolis, producers Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis had the track ready, which Janet immediately liked for its funky sound.

The music video had some memorable cameos, such as choreographer Paula Abdul as Janet's friend. Directed by Brian Jones and Piers Ashworth and choreographed by Abdul, the video only enhanced the high-energy nature of the song. As mentioned earlier, the song was a massive commercial success. In 1987, it was nominated for two Grammy Awards (Best Female R&B Vocal Performance and Best R&B Song). The song peaked at number four on the US Billboard Hot 100 and reached number one on the Hot Black Singles chart. Jimmy Jam felt that the song was the ideal opening for the album—it's sparse, funky, and, of course, Janet, the new boss. This single and the Control album were key changes in Jackson's career, making her an artist with her own voice and a clear identity.

For me, this song falls into the bucket of songs that I subconsciously know, but don’t recognize until the chorus. As soon as I heard the chorus, I had flashbacks to car rides to soccer practice in high school with my mother on aux. It has such an iconic sound and chorus, that it just has this sense of familiarity for me. However, despite being familiar with it, I never took the time to interpret and understand the message Janet Jackson was trying to convey. Now listening to it from a new perspective, I find it so interesting how I’m still able to appreciate the beauty of the sound but also the meaning and message Jackson tells.
